The growing availability of large-scale, multimodal neuroimaging datasets spanning structural MRI (sMRI), functional MRI (fMRI), and Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) offers unprecedented opportunities to characterize the diversity of brain aging trajectories and neurodegenerative processes at the individual level. AI-driven approaches can uncover complex, non-linear patterns of brain heterogeneity across these complementary modalities that would otherwise remain inaccessible through conventional analysis. However, the heterogeneity observed across patients, imaging modalities, and acquisition sites poses significant challenges for identifying clinically meaningful subgroups and ensuring the generalizability of computational models. Our research focuses on the development of deep learning frameworks, including Graph Neural Networks and unsupervised learning methods, for data-driven subgroup discovery in multimodal brain connectome data, aiming to uncover distinct neuroimaging signatures associated with cognitive decline and neurodegeneration. By extending these approaches to cognitively healthy populations, we also seek to detect subtle, pre-clinical brain changes that may precede diagnosis. Emphasis is placed on interpretability, bias mitigation, and longitudinal modeling, with the goal of producing transparent, fair, and clinically informative characterizations of disease heterogeneity across diverse populations.
